1. THE DESIGN DECISION THAT SHAPES EVERYTHING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TDS is structurally different from AC-5 / AC-6. Those were isolated engines —
new tables, new RPCs, nothing existing changed. Withholding, by contrast, sits
inside an existing money flow, so the naive implementation edits
confirm_vendor_payment or confirm_vendor_bill: the two most entangled RPCs in
the codebase (PDC, reopen and edit paths all reach into them).

APPROVED ALTERNATIVE — STANDALONE DEDUCTION DOCUMENT. TDS posts as its own
isolated entry:

    Bill      Dr Expense 10,000 / Cr AP           10,000    (unchanged)
    TDS doc   Dr AP         200 / Cr TDS Payable     200    (this migration)
    Payment   Dr AP       9,800 / Cr Bank          9,800    (unchanged)

The AP balance therefore reflects what is actually owed to the vendor AFTER
withholding, and the payment naturally settles the reduced amount. Correct
accounting, ZERO changes to any existing posting RPC.

Cost: deduction is a separate step rather than automatic. Mitigation (AC-7B):
prompt it from the bill — "194C @2% -> deduct 200", one click.

3. THREE THINGS WORTH CALLING OUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(a) A TRIPWIRE ENFORCES THE DESIGN. One regression test asserts that
    confirm_vendor_bill and confirm_vendor_payment contain no reference to TDS
    or account 2320. If a future change starts withholding inside them, the
    suite fails loudly instead of silently double-deducting.

(b) RATES ARE DATA, NOT LAW. Indian withholding rates change with each Finance
    Act. The section master is seeded with post-Budget-2024 defaults, is fully
    editable per company, and carries effective_from so a rate change is
    representable. The migration says explicitly: VERIFY THE SEEDED DEFAULTS
    AGAINST THE CURRENT FINANCE ACT before relying on them. Better a visible
    instruction than a hidden hardcoded assumption.

(c) THE STATUTORY EDGE CASES ARE ENCODED, not left to the user:
      * §206AA — no PAN means the HIGHER of the section rate and 20%
        (a section already above 20% is not reduced to 20).
      * §197 — a lower/nil-deduction certificate takes precedence over both the
        section rate and the no-PAN penalty. A 0% certificate is honoured as
        genuinely nil, not mistaken for "absent".
      * Thresholds — liability arises when EITHER the single-transaction OR the
        annual-aggregate threshold is crossed; a section with no thresholds
        always deducts.
